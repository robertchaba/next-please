# Game facts

- The player is an occupational medicine doctor who decides whether patients are fit for work.
- The location and country are unspecified.
- The game begins on 3 November 1983. A desk calendar beside the paperclip holder shows the current date.
- The doctor's key tool for issuing decisions is a mechanical rubber stamp.
- The doctor's consulting room is number 04.
- The doctor calls the next patient with a bell on the left side of the desk.
- Patient order is randomized; the first day has no fixed opening sequence.
- At most 33% of patients in a newly arranged daily queue are unfit for work. This also applies to every initial portion of that queue, so shorter working days retain the same limit. Examination criteria and the currently applicable document rules determine each patient's decision. The scripted second patient on the sixth working day is an exception to the prefix limit. Patients returning from additional examinations continue existing visits; their established findings are retained rather than balanced again.
- New patients are admitted from 08:00 to 14:00. The player arrives shortly before 08:00; the clock continues advancing during work after admissions end.
- A single click picks up an item, which follows the cursor; the next click puts it down or attempts to use it. Stamps can only be put away in their dedicated places.
- Documents and books shrink when picked up from either desktop, then keep their compact size while carried; placing them on the right desktop enlarges them for reading. Stamps are the exception and grow slightly when picked up.
- Documents on the right may overlap the consulting room, provided at least half of each sheet remains over the desktop.
- The player may stamp documents before conducting examinations and can make mistakes in handling a patient's case.
- A visit ends when the doctor hands the stamped certificate to the patient. Leaving it in the tray does not end the visit.
- Time passes continuously in the consulting room, independently of the additional time charged for actions. With a patient present, 30 real seconds equal 30 in-game minutes; with the room empty, 30 real seconds equal 5 in-game minutes. Thinking, reading the rulebook and organizing documents do not stop this flow; the rate depends on whether a patient is present.
- Books can be read and their pages turned while they lie on the desk.
- Books and multipage documents have covers; the first page turn opens the cover. The rulebook stays closed when placed on the desk.
- Patients speak sparingly, using small comic speech balloons. Not everyone greets the doctor; patients refused fitness for work generally leave without speaking.
- The clinic has a staff room where doctors and nurses talk over coffee during a break.

- Each patient portrait has five alternative identities, with names, occupations and ages selected to suit its illustrated appearance. Each identity keeps its occupation and birth date across medical variants. A portrait appears at most once in a daily queue.
- Selected patient identities have foreign-sounding names; women in this group have two-part foreign–Polish surnames. Names do not establish the country of the clinic.
- Patient birth dates are consistent across case variants and match the intended age of their portraits. Medical notes include age calculated from birth date on the date of the visit.
- Patients bring occupational-health referrals from specific employers. Referrals distinguish initial and periodic examinations and include birth date, sex, job, work conditions, and managerial responsibility.
- A scale with a height measure provides the patient's weight and height.
- Clicking the patient with an empty hand performs a general examination. It records an absence of concerns, one of 18 specific physical findings, or the occupation-specific concern about mental fitness or suspected pneumoconiosis. The finding stays the same on repeated examination of that case and is named in the notes. Abnormal general findings require refusal unless the applicable specialist rule resolves that specific concern. The specialist exception introduced on the fourth working day concerns hypertension only. From the seventh working day, a relevant additional examination can resolve doubts about a militia officer’s mental fitness or a miner’s suspected pneumoconiosis; unrelated contraindications still require refusal.
- Repeated blood pressure measurements vary slightly. Notes retain the first two measurements without overwriting them on later attempts; the latest result is still shown briefly. Blood pressure is recorded as measured values for the doctor to interpret. Very high values are written in red. The rulebook explains how to interpret the readings.
- Examinations can be performed as soon as the patient arrives, before taking or reading the referral. Findings remain available for existing or later-fetched notes. Every examination briefly shows a concise result or comment in a speech balloon.
- Vision results record a separate value for each eye. Only a significant defect disqualifies a worker in a position requiring a vision examination. Militia officers and pilots attend in uniform.
- The certificate communicates only approval or refusal, alongside the patient's identity and job position. Examination findings belong in separate medical notes written by the doctor.
- Certificate forms start with empty name and job-position fields. The doctor fills both together using the pen corner. A certificate must have both fields filled and be stamped before it is handed back.
- Medical notes initially contain only the patient’s first name, surname and age. Sheets taken without a patient remain blank until assigned to a visit. Completed examinations add their label and value automatically, directly below the previous entry, in examination order. Unperformed examinations leave no reserved rows. The general (physical) examination adds the patient history automatically. Medical notes have no pen control. Every later entry is appended below the existing entries, including a second blood pressure reading and telephone conclusions; earlier entries never move. Handwritten entries use a different ink color from printed or typewritten text.
- A four-slot document rack on the right, above the stamps, provides blank sheets and certificate forms separately. The first two slots are active initially. The third slot from the top becomes stocked with additional-examination referral forms on the seventh working day; the fourth remains reserved. A holder beside the telephone on the right provides paperclips. The doctor can attach a paperclip to a document.
- The patient brings a referral and, from the fourth working day, may also bring a specialist certificate concerning hypertension. The doctor fetches the certificate form from the rack; each sheet is carried individually from its tray or rack slot onto the main desk.
- Paperwork can be kept in the desk drawer, retrieved, or discarded in the wastebasket. The drawer can be opened throughout the working day and fills the screen. Documents lie in insertion order, overlapping as the pile grows; other stored objects lie separately. Selecting an item closes the drawer and takes it out. Clicking the lower edge closes it without taking anything.
- Formal documents carry issue dates, seals and signatures. The refusal stamp reads ODMOWA instead of NIEZDOLNY and is not limited to medical grounds.
- Dropping a sheet onto a paperclip attached to another sheet joins the documents into one movable bundle. Clicking the attached clip separates the documents.
- From the fourth working day, clicking the desk telephone gives an exchange tone. Clicking a telephone number on a document plays rotary dialing and connects the doctor to its issuer; the short conversation adds its conclusion to the medical notes.
- A detected mistaken approval brings a letter from ZUS; a detected mistaken refusal brings a letter from the employer. Letters appear after a short delay, once the patient has left. The doctor can open the envelope, read the letter, and discard it.
- ZUS, employers and the Medical Chamber use visibly different seals on their documents.
- Their signed letters use varied administrative wording about proceedings, charges and insurance, rather than directly explaining the doctor's mistake.

## Work and household budget

- New admissions end at 14:00. The last admitted patient may continue examinations and paperwork afterwards; that patient's departure ends the working day without another call. At 17:00 a cleaner calls “zamykamy już!” from the corridor. At 17:15 the clinic closes and any unfinished visit is interrupted, with no completed-visit bonus or decision penalty. Existing notes and referrals remain.
- After work, the doctor goes home, may encounter a special event, and reviews a brief household budget before going to work again.
- The household budget shows base income, patient bonus, money from the previous day, living costs, fines, insurance and any additional expenses, without detailed explanations.
- Friday, Saturday and Sunday share one budget summary before Monday. It combines income and expenses from all three days and shows the balance from before the weekend.
- Weekend domestic scenes may differ. A special event may be a trip to an unnamed mountain or seaside resort.
- The daily wage is 1000 zł, plus 50 zł for every completed appointment.
- Ordinary daily living costs apply from the first day and are randomly selected from 400–800 zł. Insurance starts at 300 zł, with penalties and additional expenses accounted for separately.
- Across Friday, Saturday and Sunday, insurance is charged once, in Friday's settlement. Saturday and Sunday pay no wages or patient bonus and charge no further insurance. The insurance rate still falls on days without a new increase.
- A weekend may consume savings but must not create or deepen debt. If the doctor cannot afford ordinary weekend living costs, 800 zł covers a modest Friday–Sunday together, including Friday's already paid living costs. Remaining charges are limited to available cash. When savings suffice, ordinary daily living costs and eligible extra expenses still apply.
- A mistaken refusal of a healthy worker goes unnoticed in 25% of ordinary cases and 3% of managerial cases.
- A mistaken approval despite contraindications goes unnoticed in 35% of cases. Each detected approval receives a ZUS fine: 100 zł for the first that day, 400 zł for each further case.
- A detected mistaken refusal increases insurance by 50 zł when the premium is 300 zł, or 100 zł otherwise. The increased premium persists. Every day without a new increase reduces it by 50 zł, down to a minimum of 300 zł.
- A balance below zero after the daily settlement ends the game in bankruptcy. A balance of exactly zero does not end the game. The doctor takes a job in the clinic cloakroom, issuing tokens instead of medical certificates. The ending allows a new game from the beginning. If the doctor is already facing the assault ending, that ending takes precedence.
- Savings above 2000 zł increase the likelihood of unexpected expenses that consume savings. Examples include medicine, a fur coat and car repairs. The recipient of any fur coat is unspecified.

## Gradually increasing formalities

- New duties arrive in official letters at the start of the third, fourth, fifth and seventh **working** days. Weekends do not advance this progression. Earlier duties remain in force when later ones begin.
- On the third working day, ZUS instructs the doctor to check the referral's issue date. A referral more than three calendar days old requires refusal; exactly three days is still valid. Dates are drawn relative to the current date, and about 5% of presented referrals are older than three days. Approving an expired referral is subject to the normal ZUS fine, with a 35% chance of no reaction.
- On the fourth working day, a sealed letter from the Medical Chamber permits conditional approval of patients with hypertension when a specialist certifies ongoing treatment and no contraindications for the specific job under review. This does not excuse other medical contraindications or invalid referrals.
- About 25% of patients with hypertension bring a specialist certificate. One third of these certificates name a different job and are invalid. The certificate carries the specialist's telephone number; calling clarifies its validity, and the conclusion is recorded in the medical notes.
- On the fifth working day, ZUS adds a duty to check referral authenticity. A clear employer seal means a valid referral; a missing seal means an invalid referral requiring refusal. An unclear seal has equal chances of being authentic or inauthentic. Referrals now carry the employer's personnel-department telephone number, and calling resolves the uncertainty. An authenticity mistake is subject to the normal ZUS fine, with a 35% chance of no reaction.
- On the sixth working day, the second patient offers a bribe of 100 zł, using the old banknote bearing Ludwik Waryński. The patient wants approval despite blood pressure or eyesight just beyond the permitted threshold.
- From this event onward, about 10% of patients (approximately one third of those unfit for work) offer 100 zł and have such borderline medical contraindications. Approving a bribing patient carries a 30% risk of a ZUS reaction and the normal fine.
- Returning the 100 zł and then refusing the patient brings no consequence; the completed appointment still earns the normal patient bonus.
- Keeping the bribe and then issuing a refusal leads to the first ending: the doctor is beaten on the way home and hospitalized. The game ends with the news that, after treatment, the doctor will also need an occupational-health examination.

- At the start of the seventh working day, the Medical Chamber announces additional examinations: tonal audiometry for steelworkers, a vestibular examination for pilots, a psychiatric consultation for militia officers with diagnosed disturbances or doubts about mental fitness for service, and a chest X-ray for miners with radiological changes suggesting pneumoconiosis.
- The doctor takes an additional-examination referral from the third rack slot from the top, circles exactly one appropriate examination and hands the referral to the patient. The patient leaves for the examination while their existing paperwork remains with the doctor; the next patient may then be called.
- Referred patients return with a report later the same day, after several hours and several intervening patients, or on the next working day. The report clearly states whether the relevant finding allows approval or requires refusal. Blood pressure, eyesight, other findings and applicable document rules must still be checked.
- Referring a patient for an examination reserved for a different occupation always produces a ZUS fine, with no chance of avoiding detection.
- The patient cast includes ten new militia-officer portraits, five new pilot portraits and ten further civilian portraits. The militia and pilot portraits retain their uniform-compatible occupation across alternative identities.
