# Game facts

Proper names below use their Polish forms. Other language versions may use consistent, nonliteral equivalents for the same people, places and institutions; this does not establish the country of the clinic.

- The player is an occupational medicine doctor who decides whether patients are fit for work.
- The location and country are unspecified. In 1983, an unnamed communist party governs the country. The opposition is the trade union Wzajemność.
- The game begins on 3 November 1983. A desk calendar beside the paperclip holder shows the current date.
- The doctor's key tool for issuing decisions is a mechanical rubber stamp.
- The doctor's consulting room is number 04.
- The doctor calls the next patient with a bell on the left side of the desk.
- Patient order is randomized; the first day has no fixed opening sequence.
- At most 33% of patients in a newly arranged daily queue fall into the original refusal pool, including cases now left to medical judgement. This also applies to every initial portion of that queue, so shorter working days retain the same limit. Examination criteria and the currently applicable document rules determine each patient's decision. The scripted second patient on the sixth working day is an exception to the prefix limit. Patients returning from additional examinations continue existing visits; their established findings are retained rather than balanced again.
- On the first day, the doctor arrives at 07:57. From the second working day onward, arrival is randomly 14–21 minutes before 08:00 (07:39–07:46). Ringing before 07:55 calls nobody because the waiting room is still empty. New patients can be called from 07:55 until 14:00; the first day retains its 08:00 first admission. The clock continues advancing during work after admissions end.
- Each working day, the newspaper “Kurier Obywatelski” is on the doctor’s desk. It is a regional daily covering a large unnamed city and its surroundings. Unfolding it on the right takes up a large area, like the rulebook. Each issue contains 3–4 articles, generally without illustrations. Background articles are chosen randomly; at least one third of the background catalog may be reused when fresh material runs out. Future story articles may replace part or all of an issue. Approximately 75% of issues also contain one classified-advertisements page with at least three ads; the page may be partly empty. No more than 25% of the classified catalog includes a telephone number; an issue may contain no phone contacts. Other ads usually invite a message in person, for example with a porter; numbered reply boxes are uncommon. Calls are available from the first working day. Attempts to call produce a random generic response, such as “Już nieaktualne.” or “(nikt nie odbiera)”. The player may store newspapers in either drawer, retrieve older issues, or discard them in the wastebasket. Retained issues keep their original date and articles across working days. A new issue arrives independently of retained or discarded copies.
- A single click picks up an item, which follows the cursor; the next click puts it down or attempts to use it. Stamps can only be put away in their dedicated places.
- Documents and books shrink when picked up from either desktop, then keep their compact size while carried; placing them on the right desktop enlarges them for reading. Stamps are the exception and grow slightly when picked up.
- Documents on the right may overlap the consulting room, provided at least half of each sheet remains over the desktop.
- The player may stamp documents before conducting examinations and can make mistakes in handling a patient's case.
- A visit ends when the doctor hands the stamped certificate to the patient. Leaving it in the tray does not end the visit.
- Time passes continuously in the consulting room, independently of the additional time charged for actions. With a patient present, 30 real seconds equal 30 in-game minutes; with the room empty, 30 real seconds equal 5 in-game minutes. Thinking, reading the rulebook and organizing documents do not stop this flow; the rate depends on whether a patient is present.
- Books can be read and their pages turned while they lie on the desk.
- Books and multipage documents have covers; the first page turn opens the cover. The rulebook stays closed when placed on the desk. The newspaper unfolds immediately when placed on the right; turning its pages does not change its size.
- Patients speak sparingly, using small comic speech balloons. Not everyone greets the doctor; patients refused fitness for work generally leave without speaking.
- The clinic has a staff room where doctors and nurses talk over coffee during a break.

- Each patient portrait has five alternative identities, with names, occupations and ages selected to suit its illustrated appearance. Each identity keeps its occupation and birth date across medical variants. A portrait appears at most once in a daily queue. Across working days, new queues prioritize faces the doctor has not yet seen, followed by those seen longest ago. Returns from additional examinations continue an existing encounter; the scripted sixth-day bribe retains its priority.
- Selected patient identities have foreign-sounding names; women in this group have two-part surnames, with a Polish second part in the Polish version and its localized equivalent in English. Names do not establish the country of the clinic.
- Patient birth dates are consistent across case variants and match the intended age of their portraits. Medical notes include age calculated from birth date on the date of the visit.
- Patients bring occupational-health referrals from specific employers. Referrals distinguish initial and periodic examinations and include birth date, sex, job, work conditions, and managerial responsibility.
- A scale with a height measure provides the patient's weight and height.
- Clicking the patient with an empty hand performs a general examination. It records an absence of concerns, one of 18 specific physical findings, or the occupation-specific concern about mental fitness or suspected pneumoconiosis. The finding stays the same on repeated examination of that case and is named in the notes. Abnormal general findings require refusal unless the applicable specialist rule resolves that specific concern. The specialist exception introduced on the fourth working day concerns hypertension only. From the seventh working day, a relevant additional examination can resolve doubts about a militia officer’s mental fitness or a miner’s suspected pneumoconiosis; unrelated contraindications still require refusal.
- Repeated blood pressure measurements vary slightly. Notes retain the first two measurements without overwriting them on later attempts; the latest result is still shown briefly. Blood pressure is recorded as measured values for the doctor to interpret. Very high values are written in red. The rulebook explains how to interpret the readings.
- Examinations can be performed as soon as the patient arrives, before taking or reading the referral. Findings remain available for existing or later-fetched notes. Every examination briefly shows a concise result or comment in a speech balloon. Normal-result comments have several variants and never repeat on consecutive normal examinations, including across patients.
- Vision results record a separate value for each eye. Only a defect greater than 6 diopters in either eye disqualifies a worker in a position requiring a vision examination. Exactly 6 diopters, regardless of sign, leaves the decision to the doctor if the other eye and other findings do not independently require refusal. Militia officers and pilots attend in uniform.
- The certificate communicates only approval or refusal, alongside the patient's identity and job position. Examination findings belong in separate medical notes written by the doctor.
- Certificate forms start with empty name and job-position fields. The doctor fills both together using the pen corner. A certificate must have both fields filled and be stamped before it is handed back.
- Medical notes initially contain only the patient’s first name, surname and age. Sheets taken without a patient remain blank until assigned to a visit. Completed examinations add their label and value automatically, directly below the previous entry, in examination order. Unperformed examinations leave no reserved rows. The general (physical) examination adds the patient history automatically. Medical notes have no pen control. Every later entry is appended below the existing entries, including a second blood pressure reading, telephone conclusions and the findings from additional examinations when the patient returns; earlier entries never move. Each additional finding remains in the medical notes after its separate report is handed to the patient, including when several additional examinations were performed. Handwritten entries use a different ink color from printed or typewritten text.
- A four-slot document rack on the right, above the stamps, provides blank sheets and certificate forms separately. The first two slots are active initially. The third slot from the top becomes stocked with additional-examination referral forms on the seventh working day; the fourth remains reserved. A holder beside the telephone on the right provides paperclips. The doctor can attach a paperclip to a document.
- The patient brings a referral and, from the fourth working day, may also bring a specialist certificate concerning hypertension. The doctor fetches the certificate form from the rack; each sheet is carried individually from its tray or rack slot onto the main desk.
- The desk has two identical drawers, with the second between the first and the wastebasket. The player decides which drawer holds each document, clipped bundle, newspaper, rulebook, loose paperclip or banknote. Paperwork can be retrieved from either drawer or discarded in the wastebasket. Either drawer can be opened throughout the working day and fills the screen. Documents are browsed through covers in filing order. A clipped bundle appears once. Patient names appear only on hover or keyboard focus, and no document preview is shown; other stored objects lie separately. Selecting an item closes the drawer and takes it out. Clicking the lower edge closes it without taking anything.
- The referral and medical note from every completed visit must both be archived in either desk drawer, including across working days.
- Formal documents carry issue dates, seals and signatures. The refusal stamp reads ODMOWA instead of NIEZDOLNY and is not limited to medical grounds.
- Dropping a sheet near a paperclip attached to another sheet joins the documents into one movable bundle. The medical note and referral have the same size and lie aligned in a bundle, with the note on top regardless of which sheet was added first. Clicking the attached clip separates the documents.
- From the first working day, clicking the desk telephone gives an exchange tone and numbers in classified ads can be dialed. From the fourth working day, clicking a telephone number on a specialist document plays rotary dialing and connects the doctor to its issuer; the short conversation adds its conclusion to the medical notes.
- Doctors address fellow doctors as “kolego” in Polish dialogue, including telephone verification of specialist certificates.
- A detected mistaken approval brings a letter from ZUS; a detected mistaken refusal brings a letter from the employer. Letters appear after a short delay, once the patient has left. The doctor can open the envelope, read the letter, and discard it.
- ZUS, employers and the Medical Chamber use visibly different seals on their documents.
- Their signed letters use varied administrative wording about proceedings, records and required duties, without describing sanctions or the consequences of failing to comply.

## Medical judgement in borderline cases

- When vision is required (W or WC), a defect of exactly 6 diopters is discretionary; a larger defect in either eye still requires refusal.
- When blood pressure is required (C or WC), readings within 5 mmHg on either side of 160 systolic or 100 diastolic are borderline, including the endpoints. Either component can establish the borderline case; systolic above 165 or diastolic above 105 still requires refusal unless the established hypertension exception applies.
- Specialist reports, including audiometry, vestibular examinations, psychiatric opinions and chest X-rays, can be equivocal and leave the final decision to the examining doctor. The report must actually be received; an outstanding referral alone does not resolve a concern. An equivocal psychiatric or chest-X-ray report leaves the corresponding militia/miner concern to the doctor's judgement.
- In a discretionary case, both approval and refusal are accepted. Neither decision itself causes a ZUS or employer complaint, fine or insurance increase. This remains true for managerial jobs and genuine referrals with unclear seals. Separate unequivocal contraindications and formal duties still apply, including valid referrals, appropriate additional tests, report delivery and records retention.
- Ambiguous findings are uncommon: approximately 20% of cases in the former refusal pool of newly arranged queues. Existing borderline readings count towards that share. Returns retain their established findings.
- The rulebook conveys this indirectly: at borderline values or with an equivocal specialist opinion, the doctor considers the patient history, general health and working conditions. It does not disclose immunity from decision consequences or the frequency of such cases. These cases can support narrative choices without a mechanically preferred approval or refusal.

## ZUS records inspections

- From the second working day, a ZUS inspector may arrive instead of the next patient, with an average frequency of one inspection per 25 completed patient visits. There are three inspector appearances.
- The inspector presents an official identity card for inspection. The doctor must return it before the records request is made.
- The inspector requests the referral and medical note for one randomly selected visit from the last 25 completed visits. This history crosses working days and does not depend on whether its documents were kept. The requested patient and exact visit date identify the required records.
- Documents may be presented separately or clipped together. Checked originals remain with the doctor for filing again. Both required documents conclude the inspection without a fine.
- Clicking the inspector with empty hands asks whether the doctor confirms missing records. The doctor may continue searching; another click confirms the loss and ends the inspection. Neither document incurs a 200 zł fine; one missing document incurs 50 zł. The inspector hands over a printed penalty notice, and the fine is included once in the daily budget.
- Inspections do not count as completed patient visits and earn no patient bonus.
- After concluding the inspection, the inspector thanks the doctor for cooperating and says goodbye before leaving, whether or not a fine was issued.

## Work and household budget

- New admissions end at 14:00. The last admitted patient may continue examinations and paperwork afterwards; that patient's departure ends the working day without another call. At 17:00 a cleaner calls “zamykamy już!” from the corridor. At 17:15 the clinic closes and any unfinished visit is interrupted, with no completed-visit bonus or decision penalty. Existing notes and referrals remain.
- After work, the doctor goes home, may encounter a special event, and reviews a brief household budget before going to work again.
- The household budget shows base income, patient bonus, net bribe income, money from the previous day, living costs, fines, insurance, any additional expenses and the closing balance, without detailed explanations. Expenses appear in red with a minus sign. Accepted bribes remain as banknotes on the desk or in the drawer after the patient leaves. They can be moved between those places and become income once at the end of the day; returned money reduces bribe income.
- Friday, Saturday and Sunday share one illustrated weekend scene with an appropriate comment, followed by one budget summary before Monday. It combines income and expenses from all three days and shows the balance from before the weekend.
- Weekend domestic scenes may differ. A special event may be a trip to an unnamed mountain or seaside resort. At most one such trip can occur during a weekend; other unexpected expenses may still occur.
- Weekend scenery and comments follow the current season in the game. A sunny golden-leaf park walk belongs to autumn and does not appear in December; winter seaside trips have winter scenery.
- The daily wage is 1000 zł, plus 50 zł for every completed appointment.
- Ordinary daily living costs apply from the first day and are randomly selected from 400–800 zł. Insurance starts at 300 zł, with penalties and additional expenses accounted for separately.
- Across Friday, Saturday and Sunday, insurance is charged once, in Friday's settlement. Saturday and Sunday pay no wages or patient bonus and charge no further insurance. The insurance rate still falls on days without a new increase.
- If the doctor cannot afford ordinary weekend living costs, a modest Friday–Sunday costs 800 zł in total, including Friday's already paid living costs. This required living cost is not capped by available cash: if savings plus Friday's income cannot cover 800 zł plus insurance and penalties, bankruptcy occurs when a daily settlement first takes the balance below zero, including on Saturday or Sunday. A balance of exactly zero remains playable. When savings suffice, ordinary daily living costs and eligible extra expenses still apply.
- A mistaken refusal of a healthy worker goes unnoticed in 25% of ordinary cases and 3% of managerial cases.
- A mistaken approval despite contraindications goes unnoticed in 35% of cases. Each detected approval receives a ZUS fine: 100 zł for the first that day, 400 zł for each further case.
- A detected mistaken refusal or approval without the required attached additional-examination report increases insurance by 50 zł when the premium is 300 zł, or 100 zł otherwise. The increased premium persists. Every day without a new increase reduces it by 50 zł, down to a minimum of 300 zł.
- A balance below zero after the daily settlement ends the game in bankruptcy. A balance of exactly zero does not end the game. The doctor takes a job in the clinic cloakroom, issuing tokens instead of medical certificates. The ending allows a new game from the beginning. If the doctor is already facing the assault ending, that ending takes precedence.
- Savings above 2000 zł increase the likelihood of unexpected expenses that consume savings. Examples include medicine, a fur coat and car repairs. The recipient of any fur coat is unspecified.

## Gradually increasing formalities

- The initial rulebook covers the procedures needed at the start, without explaining duties introduced on later working days. As bureaucracy becomes more complex, new rules and exceptions may be communicated in annexes, errata and other supplements.
- New duties arrive in official letters at the start of the third, fourth, fifth and seventh **working** days. Weekends do not advance this progression. Earlier duties remain in force when later ones begin.
- On the third working day, ZUS instructs the doctor to check the referral's issue date. A referral more than three calendar days old requires refusal; exactly three days is still valid. Dates are drawn relative to the current date, and about 5% of presented referrals are older than three days. Approving an expired referral is subject to the normal ZUS fine, with a 35% chance of no reaction.
- On the fourth working day, a sealed letter from the Medical Chamber permits conditional approval of patients with hypertension when a specialist certifies ongoing treatment and no contraindications for the specific job under review. This does not excuse other medical contraindications or invalid referrals.
- About 25% of patients with hypertension bring a specialist certificate. One third of these certificates name a different job and are invalid. The certificate carries the specialist's telephone number; calling clarifies its validity, and the conclusion is recorded in the medical notes.
- On the fifth working day, ZUS adds a duty to check referral authenticity. A clear employer seal means a valid referral; a missing seal means an invalid referral requiring refusal. An unclear seal has equal chances of being authentic or inauthentic. Referrals now carry the employer's personnel-department telephone number, and calling resolves the uncertainty. An authenticity mistake is subject to the normal ZUS fine, with a 35% chance of no reaction.
- On the sixth working day, the second patient offers a bribe of 100 zł, using the old banknote bearing Ludwik Waryński. The patient wants approval despite blood pressure or eyesight just beyond the permitted threshold.
- From this event onward, about 10% of patients (approximately one third of those unfit for work) offer 100 zł and have such borderline medical contraindications. Approving a bribing patient despite unequivocal contraindications carries a 30% risk of a ZUS reaction and the normal fine. The medical-judgement rule also applies to borderline findings in these visits; a bribe does not change their medical assessment.
- Returning the 100 zł and then refusing the patient brings no consequence; the completed appointment still earns the normal patient bonus.
- Keeping the bribe and then issuing a refusal leads to the first ending: the doctor is beaten on the way home and hospitalized. The game ends with the news that, after treatment, the doctor will also need an occupational-health examination.

- Pilots, militia officers, miners and steelworkers first join newly arranged patient queues on the seventh working day, when additional examinations become available.
- At the start of the seventh working day, the Medical Chamber announces additional examinations: tonal audiometry for steelworkers, a vestibular examination for pilots, a psychiatric consultation for militia officers with diagnosed disturbances or doubts about mental fitness for service, and a chest X-ray for miners with radiological changes suggesting pneumoconiosis.
- The doctor takes an additional-examination referral from the third rack slot from the top, circles exactly one appropriate examination and hands the referral to the patient. The patient leaves for the examination while their existing paperwork remains with the doctor; the next patient may then be called.
- Referred patients return with a report later the same day, after several hours and several intervening patients, or on the next working day. The report usually states whether the relevant finding allows approval or requires refusal; an equivocal report instead leaves this assessment to the doctor. Blood pressure, eyesight, other findings and applicable document rules must still be checked.
- When a worker returns from additional specialist examinations and is approved for work, the doctor must hand over the certificate paperclipped to that worker’s examination report. Omitting the attached report always brings a reprimand letter from militia headquarters, the LOT office or the workplace, according to the worker’s employment, and an insurance increase (100% detection). A refusal does not require the report, but the worker also accepts a refusal certificate paperclipped to it.
- Referring a patient for an examination reserved for a different occupation always produces a ZUS fine, with no chance of avoiding detection.
- The patient cast includes ten new militia-officer portraits, five new pilot portraits and ten further civilian portraits. The militia and pilot portraits retain their uniform-compatible occupation across alternative identities.
