# Portable save format

Saves are local `.npsave` files containing one gzip member with UTF-8 JSON. They are neither encrypted nor uploaded. Export uses `next-please-day-N.npsave`; import checks bytes rather than trusting filenames or MIME types. Only locale and audio preferences use localStorage. Import replaces live state only after validation succeeds, preserving all live gameplay and presentation on failure.

## Version 36: complaint grounds

Exports write version 36. Ordinary letters may carry an optional `concern`: `expired-referral`, `invalid-referral`, `identity-mismatch`, `general`, `additional-test`, `vision`, `pressure`, `authentic-referral`, `unjustified-refusal` or `wrong-test`. The value is captured when the detected mistake creates its letter, independently of retained clinical paperwork. It selects a localized clue, never the decision, detection or penalty. When several issues coexist, the existing assessment order chooses the first: expiry, authenticity, identity, general findings, additional findings, vision, pressure. A mistaken refusal of an otherwise eligible patient with an unclear genuine seal records `authentic-referral`; other mistaken refusals record `unjustified-refusal`.

Only letters accept the field; it cannot coexist with `reason: "missing-result"`. `unjustified-refusal` requires the employer sender, all other concerns require ZUS. Date, authenticity and additional-test concerns require their respective working-day gates, and identity mismatch requires the established I1 case. Unknown/non-string values and extra fields fail atomically. The v36 validator strips validated concerns before applying all frozen v35 checks, then restores them to copied letters. No other state, limit or RNG stream changes.

Versions 1–35 keep their exact validators. Earlier letters without a concern remain generic: discarded referrals and historical findings cannot safely be reconstructed. No new concern is fabricated during migration. Existing missing-result letters retain their reason. Re-export writes v36, and failed imports leave live state and presentation intact.

## Version 35: chapter one, publications and epilogues

Version 35 adds the required `story` object (`NarrativeState`, internal `version: 1`) and the `epilogue` phase. Dates in this object are civil game days K, never working-day numbers W: W26 is K36 (8 December 1983), and continuation starts at K37. The authored catalog contains eighteen visits across ten actors. `scheduled`, `active`, `completed`, `missed` and `cancelled` statuses are distinct; an unfinished appointment never fabricates a refusal. The clinical patient catalog appends IDs 6193–6210, and explicit identity lookup preserves recurring actors independently of old arithmetic IDs. Older case IDs, names and results are unchanged.

`story.decisions` contains exact `{ eventId, encounterId, day, sequence, decision }` records. Event IDs are `decision:<encounterId>` and each encounter completes at most once. `story.warnings` stores `{ warningId, deliveredDay, deliveredSequence, documentInstanceId }`, using `audit`, `security`, `consistency` or `militant`. Allocating a physical document establishes delivery; planning a warning does not. Sequences are consecutive across decisions and warnings. `appliedEventIds` preserves that same event order and appends `resolution:chapter-1` after resolving the chapter. The party/union/suspicion scores, negligence evidence and two allowed inconsistency comparisons are derived from ordered decisions, with limits applied after each change. No separately editable score cache is serialized. Validation checks conditional chains, calendar windows, warning-trigger order, threshold crossings and the deterministic ending.

`deliveredDocumentIds` remembers actual delivered content even after its paper is discarded. `contacts` stores unique `CALL-…:<encounterId>` or `CALL-PRINT:press` keys; telephone information does not change scores or consume random draws. Case calls append one `story-call:<contactId>` entry to chronological clinical notes and the matching active or pending visit; the translated conclusion survives deleting its source letter. Validation binds each entry to a possible call about that exact case. A `story-document` paper uses the ordinary identity/date/location/opened fields and a `data` object with exactly `definitionId`, `encounterId`, `issuedDay`, `variantId`, `subjectIdentityId`, and `sourceDecisionEventIds`. All IDs belong to closed catalogs. The identity belongs to that document: the alias referral/certificate identifies Sikora while the two supplied reports identify Lis. Historic certificate H-OLD-CERT has a fixed 19 November 1982 printed date; its physical intake remains the actual chapter date. Warning and ending evidence points to real completed decision events, frozen at delivery. Imports reject future or unrelated evidence, wrong subjects, unsupported variants, premature delivery, and outcomes inconsistent with their source decision.

The printer's `additionalResult` is `{ test: "audiometry", fit: false, source: "provided" }`. The false result is explicitly discretionary for this authored case; it is not inferred from ID parity. `additionalTests` and the chronological result note record the report that actually exists. Ordinary referred results retain the historical `{ test, fit }` shape. A supplied result alone never creates a fictitious pending return when the clinic closes. Actual referrals and their returns retain the existing clinical lifecycle, including returns after the story window; late medical completion never rewrites a missed story decision.

Physical newspapers may additionally carry `id`, `publication`, `issueNumber`, `storyArticleIds`, and `storyClassifiedIds`. New delivered copies have stable keys such as `kurier:13` and `glos:13`; the two titles can independently occupy the desk, either drawer or the wastebasket. Old copies without publication fields remain the Courier. The existing `newspaper` field continues to track the separate random background pool. Story article IDs never enter that pool. Daily issues have three or four combined background/story articles; the W6 classified C-AD is guaranteed. Weekly issues have three or four authored articles, with permitted recurring background and no classifieds. Issue numbers count physically printed weekly editions, so skipped K27 does not consume a number.

`publicationHistory` stores `issue:kurier:<K>`, `issue:glos:<K>` and `<articleId>@<K>` records. Finite authored daily issues are recorded even if the player discards their copies; background-only daily issues need no new story history. `pendingUnionArticleIds` preserves the ordered unprinted comments. `unionPress` holds `firstIssueCalendarDay` (null, K13 or K20), `skippedCalendarDays` (empty or `[27]`) and `printedIssueCount`. Every weekly deadline is seven calendar days after the previous one; missing K27 never shifts K34 or K41. Validation reconstructs eligibility, publication order and exact priority-selected weekly content. It rejects a removed prerequisite, a future-aware article, reordered or forged current issue, impossible issue count, and a queue containing a published comment. Retained copies keep their selected variants across language changes and later decisions.

`resolution` is null or `{ endingId, resolvedDay, terminal, presented, variant }`. The six ending IDs are `exposed`, `clinic-closed`, `detained`, `party-continue`, `union-continue`, and `independent-continue`. `variant` is `standard`, `violations` or `inconsistency`; only clinic closure uses the latter two. An already entered assault/hospital or bankruptcy ending retains precedence. Morning exposure at K36 preserves the actual K35 ledger and cash, creates no K36 wages or newspaper, and stores the new day and epilogue explicitly. The other five endings follow the real K36 settlement. Terminal resolutions cannot continue. Nonterminal `continue-story` sets `presented`, advances once to K37 without another settlement and preserves all money, papers, inspections, suspicion and publication history. The final letter keeps `issuedDay: 36` when retained on K37.

Imports of versions 1–34 first pass their frozen historical validators and catalog ceilings. Migration adds `createStory("legacy-disabled")`; it does not replay earlier encounters, retrofit warnings, change an existing queue or reroll medical/economic state. These worlds retain their ordinary gameplay and may be exported as v35. Only a new game starts chapter one. The v35 validator checks the new closed fields first, projects them out for the established clinical/cash checks, then reconstructs independent validated records. Invalid input never replaces live state. The gzip, CRC, 1 MiB compressed and 2 MiB expanded limits remain unchanged.

## Patients, randomness and time

`src/core/patients.ts` defines a stable catalog of 6,211 fictional cases. Version 35 appends the eighteen authored visits at IDs 6193–6210 and ten fixed actors at character IDs 188–197. These actors never enter random queues; scheduled visits replace ordinary slots. The underlying ordinary catalog still contains 6,193 cases and 188 portraits. Version 30 appends discretionary variants at IDs 5253–6192, one per existing identity, without altering earlier cases (see the v30 contract below). Version 29 appends IDs 4003–5252 for 50 civilian portraits (characters 138–187), adding 250 identities and preserving all previous cases and names. Version 28 appends IDs 3378–4002 for 25 civilian portraits (characters 113–137), adding 125 identities while preserving all preceding cases and localized names. Version 25 appends IDs 3128–3377 for ten new portraits (characters 103–112), each with five identities and five clinical variants; all preceding case IDs and identities stay unchanged. IDs 553–3127 provide five clinical variants for each of 515 authored identities (five identities per portrait); IDs 2503–3127 append the 25 portraits introduced with version 19; names, birth dates, jobs and employers are fixed per identity. IDs 0–552 are retained for imports: the original 303 cases retain their immutable identities, legacy examination categories and expected decisions. IDs 103–302 provide eight variants for each of 25 added characters; IDs 303–327 append one general-examination finding for each of those characters. Those additions reuse existing portraits. IDs 328–552 append nine cases for each of 25 new portraits (`patient-51`–`patient-75`); every field of IDs 0–327 remains unchanged. Each case supplies a `characterId`, portrait, demographics, employer/job, managerial responsibility, examination kind, requirement profile and test results. Profiles distinguish vision, pressure, and combined requirements; general examination and measurements accompany every profile. Each case also supplies stable numeric `visionValues` (`left`, `right`) and `pressureValues` (`systolic`, `diastolic`), and a `general` finding (`pass` or `refer`). An abnormal general finding ordinarily requires refusal. From working day seven, a fitting psychiatric or chest-X-ray report may clear the corresponding militia/miner concern; it never clears other contraindications. Ordinary notes derive one of 18 specific localized abnormal descriptions from the immutable case; the story storekeeper has an explicit nineteenth recurrent-dizziness description. The same case and repeated examinations retain the same finding without an additional save field. Only substantial vision defects disqualify under a vision requirement; small defects remain compatible with approval. Legacy `vision`/`pressure` categories are retained internally for validation of older formats. Stored paper and visit `patientId` values identify cases, not portrait indices. Static case IDs preserve historical results across later days without storing duplicate patient snapshots. The game uses simplified fictional examination rules rather than clinical thresholds.

`roster` contains 168 valid case IDs before working day 7 and 188 from that working day onward, one per distinct eligible character. Pilots, militia officers, miners and steelworkers are excluded before their additional-examination rules arrive. One of the eligible authored identities is selected per portrait; no fixed introductory identities lead the queue. Newly generated queues cap unfit cases at 33% of every prefix, including at most 55 of 168 or 62 of 188 overall, by selecting existing fit variants of the same identity and adjusting order as needed. Balancing never changes the chosen name, profession or birth date. Its final order and chosen variants are persisted. This is a generation rule, not an import invariant: existing saved queues may exceed the cap and remain unchanged until the next workday. Version 29 also persists cross-day admission recency in `portraitHistory`, described below. Version 21 permits 1–103 unique catalog portraits in a work queue; character IDs are bounded by the catalog, independently of queue length. The empty queue remains reserved for settled weekend home days. Imported queues remain unchanged; their profession mix is not revalidated against the new generation gate. Versions 1–20 retain their historical exact schemas and length bounds; in those formats, 28- and 53-person queues limit case IDs to 0–102 or 0–327 respectively. Versions 15–18 accept IDs 0–2502 in 78-person queues; version 19 accepts IDs through 3127 and retains imported 28-, 53- and 78-person queues. Fresh queues use IDs 553–6192. Pending returns preserve their exact case and occupy their portrait’s slot instead of selecting a new identity for that face. Versions 13 and 14 keep their frozen 0–552 bounds. Duplicate `characterId` values remain invalid even when two cases refer to different identities sharing a portrait. Rest days have an empty roster and require the home phase, a Saturday/Sunday date, zero completed patients and zero salary/bonus. `rngState` is an unsigned 32-bit integer; the pure LCG transition is `next = (1664525 * seed + 1013904223) mod 2^32`, with result `next / 2^32`. Explicit updates consume draws for queues, repeated pressure measurements, detection and the household budget. Renderers consume no randomness. `createInitialState(seed)` accepts a caller-supplied seed for reproducible fixtures; omitting it obtains a fresh unsigned seed through browser `crypto.getRandomValues`, outside drawing. Saving and loading cannot reroll an existing queue, detected mistake or settled expense.

Time advances continuously as well as through actions. In an active consulting room (including its open drawer), 30 real seconds add 30 game minutes with an active visit or inspector, or 5 game minutes with an empty room. Thinking, pointer movement, reading and animation do not pause this flow. Action costs are additional. Menu panels, hidden tabs and the mobile reception pause elapsed time, without catch-up on return. The runtime accumulator retains sub-minute progress across actions, rate changes and pauses; version 17 continues to save whole minutes only. A successful import, restart or new day resets the fraction; failed imports preserve it. The elapsed clock itself adds no save fields. The clock is an integer from 459 (07:39 earliest later-day arrival) to 1035 (17:15 final closing); day one and introductory saves retain a minimum of 477 (07:57). cabinet saves stop at 1034 because reaching 1035 closes the day. Calling a patient, taking a referral, fetching/filling/returning a certificate, stamping, handling an object, turning a manual page and decorative interactions cost one minute. General and vision examinations each cost three, blood pressure four, and measurements two; repeated procedures retain these costs. Calling a patient before 07:55 leaves state unchanged. From 07:55 onward a call costs one minute; day one alone retains its minimum first admission at 08:00. Waiting, animation, pointer movement and unavailable actions add no discrete action cost. The clock continues after the 14:00 admission cutoff. The cleaner calls at 17:00; an active visit can continue only until 17:15. Elapsed time reaching 17:15 closes the clinic through the same settlement as actions. A valid action reaching 17:15 is interrupted before its effects are committed, the active visit and its certificate clip membership are removed, and the day settles from the prior state. The unfinished visit earns no patient bonus, does not increment completion counters, and causes no decision penalty or complaint. Already recorded notes and referrals remain. If this is a visit resumed after an additional examination, its complete visit and report are queued for the next working day; its certificate and findings are not lost. No new patient can be called at or after 14:00. Returning the last certificate at closing, or exhausting the queue, settles the day automatically. The view completes the patient departure before presenting home. `end-day` remains available for an empty closed cabinet, including imported older saves.

Versions 1–31 retain their minimum action-time bounds: an empty cabinet after `n` completed visits requires at least `480 + 4n − 1` (477 before the first visit); an active visit requires `480 + 4n`, plus one for referral intake, three for general examination, three for vision, four for pressure, two for measurements and one for an applied stamp. Bounds cap at 840. Migration does not invent time for certificates that were already supplied or named by older rules. A resumed visit begun on an earlier day may retain its prior examinations at 08:00; the import validator does not charge those procedures again.

## Additional examinations and continued visits (version 19)

`referralsIssued` counts specialist referrals handed out on the current day, from zero to 412, and resets with the daily budget. Referral handoff does not increment `shiftCompleted` or `patientsProcessed` and earns no bonus. `pendingReferrals` contains at most 103 continued visits. Every entry has exactly `visit`, `sentDay`, `returnDay`, `returnMinute`, `returnAfterCompleted`, `test` and `fit`. `visit` is the full validated appointment, including certificate fields, existing examination values, chronology, calls and accepted/returned cash. A patient is either active or pending, never both; pending portraits are distinct and their cases remain in the uncompleted part of the current roster. Weekend home states retain the pending list while their roster is empty.

The four `test` values are `audiometry`, `vestibular`, `psychiatry` and `chest-xray`. Each visit adds `startedDay`, `additionalTests` (a distinct list of at most four already issued test types) and `additionalResult` (null or exactly `{ "test": "vestibular", "fit": true }`). Reissuing the same test is rejected without spending time or consuming randomness. A later unrelated test does not replace a prior report relevant to the patient’s occupation. `additionalResultFor` derives the immutable case outcome; stored pending outcomes and result papers are checked against it, so import cannot reroll or forge a report. New visits begin with no tests/result. Legacy visits migrate with their current day as `startedDay`, no additional tests and no result; historical active paid weekend shifts remain valid.

A successful handoff consumes two saved RNG draws solely for scheduling. The same-day path chooses 120–180 minutes and requires three other completed visits before the return can be called. A 40% next-workday branch, or insufficient remaining admission time/patients, schedules 08:00 on the next working day. Uncalled same-day returns remain pending overnight. Saturday and Sunday never become return dates. The bell prioritizes an eligible return and moves its existing case to `roster[shiftCompleted]`; otherwise it admits the next case without a pending referral. Sending a patient moves that case later without changing its identity or findings. This reordering does not count a suspended appointment as completed.

The two new paper variants are `additional-referral`, with `patientId` and nullable `test`, and `additional-result`, with `patientId`, non-null `test` and boolean `fit`. They share the existing `id`, `day` and `location` fields. The rack supplies a form for the active visit; selecting a row replaces its sole selected test. Handoff removes only that form and detaches its clip membership, retaining the remaining documents. The patient’s certificate state is suspended with the visit. A return restores it and adds the signed report on the desk. Notes, employer referrals and specialist certificates retain their original `startedDay`; later examinations and phone conclusions update notes even when filed in the drawer. Their snapshot consistency is validated for both active and pending visits.

From working day seven, `directive` additionally accepts `rule: "additional-tests"`, with the Chamber sender, only on that rule’s actual delivery date. An examination for a different occupation always adds one ZUS case and a letter when the form is handed over. Detection is not sampled; the normal first/subsequent daily fine scale remains 100/400 zł. Multiple legitimate complaint letters for the same patient/day are supported. Ordinary decision consequences continue to use their existing detection draws.

Version 19 validates exact keys, day gates, bounded distinct test lists, deterministic reports, pending identity/roster ownership, return schedules, retained document snapshots, complete clip/location membership and all prior clinical/budget rules. It validates versions 1–18 with their historical schemas before adding empty referral state, preserving money, RNG, paperwork and queue order. In particular, versions 15–18 retain case ceiling 2502, 78 portraits and job ceiling 44 for hypertension certificates. No imported old appointment gains an invented additional test or penalty.

## ZUS inspections (version 25)

`recentVisits` is an array of at most 25 distinct `{ "patientId": 553, "day": 1 }` entries, in completion order. Each entry uses the exact case and original appointment day, including visits continued after additional examinations. Completing a certificate handoff appends an entry and drops the oldest beyond 25; interrupted visits and inspector appointments do not append anything. History survives day changes, document disposal, and import/export. There is no calendar-age limit. Current-day entries must belong to completed positions in the saved roster.

`inspectionCheckedAt` is the lifetime completed-patient count at the last eligibility draw, between zero and `patientsProcessed`. Beginning with day two, calling the bell with a nonempty history and a newly completed appointment draws a 1/25 chance from the saved RNG. A successful draw chooses one history entry uniformly and one of three inspector identities (`inspectorId: 0 | 1 | 2`). Calling repeatedly during or just after that inspection cannot reroll the same completed-patient count. The inspector consumes time but does not consume a roster entry, count as a patient, or earn a visit bonus. The first day never admits inspectors.

`inspection` is null or exactly `{ "target": { "patientId": 553, "day": 1 }, "inspectorId": 0, "stage": "identity", "idTaken": false, "received": [] }`. It can exist only in a cabinet on day two or later, from 08:00, with no clinical visit. Its target must occur in `recentVisits` and its checked count must equal `patientsProcessed`. `stage` is `identity`, `records`, or `confirm-missing`. The identity stage has no received records; later stages cannot retain the identity card. `received` contains at most one of `referral` or `note`, because providing both ends the inspection immediately. It records papers already shown; their later location or disposal does not erase the inspector's review.

Taking the identity card creates one `inspection-id` paper with exactly the common `id`, `day`, `location`, null `patientId`, and `inspectorId`. The inspector's `idTaken` flag requires exactly one corresponding card, whose day and identity match the active inspection. The card may be stored and retrieved but cannot be discarded, including as part of a bundle. Returning the card requires it on the desk, removes only that card and its clip membership, and starts the records request.

An inspection accepts only referral and note papers matching the requested case **and appointment day**. A clipped bundle may supply both; sequential handoffs also work. Reviewed originals stay on the desk and can be archived again. The first empty-hand conversation during the records phase asks whether missing records are confirmed; a second conversation ends the inspection. No supplied documents incur 200 zł, one document kind incurs 50 zł, and both incur no penalty. Providing a valid missing sheet while confirmation is open returns to review or completes the inspection. Wrong papers do not change the review or charge time.

A penalty immediately creates an `inspection-fine` paper with the common `id`, issue `day`, target `patientId`, `location`, `inspectorId`, the original `targetDay`, and `amount: 50 | 200`. The paper may be archived, clipped, retrieved, or discarded. Its removal never cancels the fine. `inspectionFines` is the daily sum of inspection penalties, in 50-zł steps, included once in `daily.fines` and the ordinary settlement. It resets on the next day. Retained fine papers for the current day cannot total more than this recorded charge. Historical weekend ledgers retain their combined fine totals, exact expense arithmetic, and closing/opening continuity.

Finishing an inspection after admissions close settles the day normally. At final clinic closure, an unfinished inspection ends without a new fine; any identity card is returned and removed from clip groups, and no patient bonus is awarded. All inspection transitions are pure and consume the same handling-minute rules as other paperwork.

Version 25 validates exact field sets, stages, target history membership, portrait bounds, card ownership, clip locations, fine amounts and totals, then applies the established clinical and settlement validators. Versions 1–24 pass their unchanged schemas before gaining an empty history, null inspection, zero inspection fines, and a checked count equal to their saved lifetime patient count; no visit history or inspection is invented. Versions 19–24 retain the 103-portrait/3128-case ceilings despite the expanded current catalog. Imported rosters, RNG, balances, paperwork, and patients remain unchanged.

## Consequences and home settlement

`daily` stores detected unsafe approvals and inappropriate specialist referrals (`zusCases`), insurance cases (`refusals`, the historical key for detected refusals of healthy workers and approvals missing a required report), cumulative `fines`, and `insuranceIncrease`. Insurance cases cannot exceed completed patients. From working day 7 in v23, ZUS cases and insurance cases may independently affect the same completed visit, so their combined bound is `2 * shiftCompleted + referralsIssued`; earlier days and older versions retain `shiftCompleted + referralsIssued`: an inappropriate specialist referral incurs a ZUS case before the visit is completed. An unsafe approval is unnoticed with probability 35%; a healthy refusal is unnoticed with probability 25%, or 3% for managers. Only detected mistakes generate complaint letters and financial consequences. A missing required attachment always produces its insurance consequence without a detection draw. Correct medical decisions consume no detection draw. A hidden mistake neither escalates fines nor increases insurance.

`fines` equals `inspectionFines` plus zero without a medical ZUS case, otherwise `100 + 400 * (zusCases - 1)`. Inspection penalties never increment `zusCases` or insurance. `insuranceRate` starts at 300, persists across days, stays in steps of 50 and is bounded by the money guardrail. Each detected healthy refusal increases it by 50 when it is 300, otherwise by 100. The recorded increase must match the refusal count and resulting rate. If no increase occurred that day, settlement first reduces the rate by 50, with a minimum of 300, then charges that reduced rate on a paid day. Saturday and Sunday keep this decay but charge zero insurance; Friday supplies the only premium for Friday–Sunday.

Closing certificate handoff, or `end-day` with no active visit and either 14:00 or an exhausted queue, computes the budget once, sets `phase: "home"`, preserves an overtime clock (at least 840) and credits the closing balance to `money`. `settlement` is null outside home. At home it contains exactly:

```json
{
  "day": 1,
  "patients": 20,
  "basePay": 1000,
  "patientBonus": 1000,
  "bribeIncome": 0,
  "income": 2000,
  "livingCost": 600,
  "insuranceCost": 300,
  "fines": 0,
  "event": null,
  "expenses": 900,
  "openingBalance": 0,
  "closingBalance": 1100,
  "weekend": null
}
```

On paid workdays, salary is 1000 zł and the bonus is 50 zł for each completed patient, including mistakes. On Saturday/Sunday rest days both are zero. Ordinary living cost is drawn uniformly from all integers 400–800 inclusive, including the first day. Saturday draws both ordinary Saturday and Sunday costs before deciding affordability. If their sum exceeds Friday’s closing balance, use the required remaining modest allowance `max(0, 800 − Friday livingCost)`; Saturday pays half rounded down and Sunday the remainder, without capping either charge by available cash. A completed modest Friday–Sunday costs exactly 800 zł. Savings plus Friday income below `800 + Friday insurance + Friday fines` cause bankruptcy at the first negative daily settlement, including Saturday or Sunday; exactly zero remains playable. If both ordinary draws fit the balance, pay them in full. Sunday uses the saved cost, without rerolling its amount. Both weekend days charge zero insurance. Ordinary living costs and optional events remain affordable, but the required modest allowance may create debt. After income, living cost, insurance and fines, savings at or below 2000 trigger no event. Above 2000, event probability is `min(0.85, 0.10 + (savings - 2000) / 10000)`. This balancing curve is an implementation parameter, not an additional fixed story rule. At most one random expense is generated per day: `medicine`, `car-repair`, `home-repair` or `fur-coat`. On rest days the same eligible pool also includes `mountain-trip` and `seaside-trip`, with no named destination; trips use the same savings threshold, probability and cost bounds. New weekends permit at most one trip. When settling Sunday, a mountain/seaside event in the immediately preceding Saturday settlement removes both trips from the eligible pool; ordinary extra expenses remain eligible. A Saturday save already contains the information needed to resume with this limit, so there is no new field or format version. Validators continue accepting already settled historical weekends with two trips and preserve all recorded events, balances and costs; importing them neither refunds expenses nor settles them again. Event cost is a uniform integer from 200 to `min(savings − reservedSundayCost, 700 + floor((savings - 2000) * 0.75))`, where the reservation is zero outside Saturday, reducing available savings without creating event-induced debt. The fur bill leaves its recipient unspecified. Further story and family/relationship details are reserved for later; bribery is defined below.

`weekend` is null on paid-day ledgers and on preserved historical ledgers. New rest-day ledgers store exactly `{ "modest": true, "sundayLivingCost": 100 }`, with a boolean mode and an integer reservation from 0 to 800. Saturday ordinary mode requires both daily costs in 400–800. Saturday modest mode requires each cost in 0–200, with Sunday equal to Saturday or one higher. Ordinary costs plus the Sunday reservation cannot exceed nonnegative opening cash. Version 22 permits modest costs and the Sunday reservation to exceed cash; a negative closing balance still requires a terminal ending. Sunday requires a zero reservation; modest living cost remains in 0–200. The mode selects the modest arrival illustration and comment in both languages. Missing or extra keys fail validation. A null historical rest ledger retains the old 400–800 living cost and the already charged insurance; it is never retroactively settled again.

`budgetHistory` is a required array of at most two complete `DaySettlement` records. It starts empty. Advancing Friday to Saturday appends Friday’s settlement, and advancing to Sunday appends Saturday’s. The current day remains in `settlement`. Only settled weekend home states with an empty roster may retain history; Saturday permits one earlier day and Sunday two, in consecutive order immediately preceding the current day. Monday clears the history. Each historical ledger passes the same exact-key, bounds, wages, expense, event and arithmetic checks as a daily ledger; its closing balance must be nonnegative and equal the next opening balance. Saturday’s modest allowance is checked against Friday’s living cost, accepting either the full remainder or the historical cash-capped remainder to preserve imported plans. Sunday must consume Saturday’s saved cost and mode without a cash cap. Rendering sums the recorded categories and event expenses without drawing randomness or charging again. Remaining weekend days settle before a single weekend scene and combined budget are presented. Terminal endings retain their existing immediate presentation.

Version 20 introduces only this history field. Versions 1–19 still pass their frozen schemas before migration with `budgetHistory: []`; no missing Friday or Saturday ledger is invented, and money, RNG, events and reservations remain unchanged. An older save loaded partway through a weekend summarizes the available current and subsequently accumulated ledgers. A Friday import can accumulate the complete three-day budget. Failed validation preserves the live state.

`event` is null or exactly `{ "kind": "car-repair", "cost": 450 }` with a valid kind/cost. Validation checks all ledger values, event bounds, matching day/patient counts/premium/fines, `income = basePay + patientBonus + bribeIncome`, `expenses = livingCost + insuranceCost + fines + eventCost`, and `closingBalance = openingBalance + income − expenses = money`. Saved home screens cannot settle again. `next-day` is available only from home. It preserves lifetime patients and stored belongings, resets daily consequences and checks for a month boundary. When the next date is Saturday/Sunday, it immediately settles that rest day once, retains home phase, uses an empty roster, charges the planned living cost with zero insurance (retaining normal daily premium decay), and saves any optional event. It creates no workday or patient income. When the next date is a weekday, it preserves money and insurance, clears the ledger, generates the eligible queue of up to 138 characters and starts the cabinet at 07:57. Guardrail failure leaves the old state intact. The UI’s page and typewriter positions remain temporary. Paid weekend shifts imported from older formats retain their salary and remain resumable; newly advanced weekend dates use the rest-day flow.

## Paperwork and presentation

Seasonal weekend illustration and caption selection is derived presentation: the existing `day`, `rngState`, modest flag and saved event ledgers determine it. The settled date (normally Sunday) supplies the season, independent of host time and language. No weather, illustration or caption field is serialized, and the version 29 schema is unchanged. Rendering and reopening a settled weekend consume no random draws and charge no costs. See [the seasonal scene catalog](art-weekends-seasonal-2026-10-30.md).

`papers` contains at most 4096 sheets. Every paper has a unique integer `id`, originating `day` no later than the current day, `patientId` and `location` (`desk`, `drawer` or `drawer-2`). IDs remain below `nextPaperId` (1–1,000,000,001), are allocated monotonically and are not reused. Shapes are:

```json
[
  { "id": 1, "kind": "referral", "day": 1, "patientId": 0, "location": "desk",
    "details": { "ageDays": 0, "seal": "clear", "authentic": true, "phone": "42-1000" } },
  { "id": 2, "kind": "note", "day": 1, "patientId": 0, "location": "drawer", "additionalWritten": true,
    "examinationOrder": ["general", "pressure", "measurements", "vision"],
    "results": { "general": "pass", "vision": { "left": 0, "right": 0 },
      "pressure": { "systolic": 108, "diastolic": 62 },
      "pressureReadings": [{ "systolic": 108, "diastolic": 62 }], "weight": 62, "height": 164 } },
  { "id": 3, "kind": "letter", "day": 1, "patientId": 0, "location": "desk", "sender": "employer", "opened": true }
]
```

Referrals are created once at intake. Certificate availability belongs to the active visit rather than an independent Paper. Blank notes have null patient/results, an empty `examinationOrder`, and `additionalWritten: false`; their sheet remains visually blank. Patient arrival, sheet intake and retrieval bind available blank desk notes to the active visit, even before any examination. Examination actions update same-day notes for that case, including notes in the drawer. Retrieving a blank note records existing examinations. The pen adds patient history once, after the general (physical) examination. Bound notes may contain identity only, with empty results/order and `additionalWritten: false`; non-pressure results must match the effective case (the immutable catalog, or the persisted bribery variant), and weight/height are both present or both null. `results.pressureReadings` preserves the first two measured values; `results.pressure` is null for an empty history or equals its final entry (the second measurement once available). Repeated measurements after the second never overwrite notes, including notes filed in the drawer or retrieved later. Numeric vision and pressure result objects have exact keys. Vision matches the effective case values; pressure readings satisfy the same bounded variation rules as the visit and are independently copied. Current-visit note histories must equal the visit’s first-two history when pressure has been recorded. `general` is null until examined, then matches the effective case’s `pass`/`refer` value. Every note’s `examinationOrder` contains exactly its non-null examinations, with no duplicates; current-visit notes also preserve their relative order from the visit and cannot claim an examination that has not occurred. The renderer writes only existing entries consecutively after the patient name, without empty examination slots. Version 18 uses the separate `noteOrder` journal described below, preserving the exact position of repeat pressure readings, history and telephone conclusions. Historical and other-patient notes remain unchanged. Referrals and letters are unique by kind/day/case; current-day paperwork must correspond to an admitted/completed position in the saved roster. At 17:15, home saves may also retain notes and referrals for the interrupted case at `roster[shiftCompleted]`; a letter still requires a completed visit. Letter sender is `zus` for erroneous approvals and for a mistaken authenticity refusal of an otherwise eligible patient with an unclear genuine seal; other erroneous refusals use `employer`; letters require a completed visit. From the third working day, document rules can change fitness independently of the static case, so a letter sender need not match the catalog's medical-only decision. Before referral intake, the active case may own bound notes and a taken specialist certificate but no referral paper. Deleting a letter never removes recorded financial consequences.

`paperClips` contains nonempty groups, e.g. `[[1, 2, "certificate"], [3]]`. Each member refers to an existing sheet or the active taken certificate, occurs only once, and shares its group’s location. Singleton groups represent one clipped sheet. Joining merges groups; unclipping removes the group record. Storing/retrieving/discarding operates on whole groups. Certificate groups cannot be stored or discarded; handoff removes only certificate membership. Storing moves papers to the end of the array in filing order. Papers, bundles and stored objects survive day transitions.

`drawerItems` contains exactly `drawer` and `drawer-2`, each holding boolean `manual` and integer `clips` (0–4096), independently of paper bundles. At most one drawer can contain the rulebook. Each store/retrieve costs one handling minute capped at 17:15; duplicate manual storage, empty retrieval and excess clips leave state/time unchanged.

Coordinates, carried footprints, loose desk clips, stacking order, selected tools, book pages, patient animations, speech and typewriter positions are presentation state. Import restores default layout and no active movement; failure preserves the existing layout, animation and state. Menus pause presentation without changing saved progress. Start over resets gameplay and invalidates pending old imports, preserving preferences and exported files.

## Working-day progression, calls and endings

`src/core/progression.ts` derives the working-day number from `day`; Saturdays and Sundays do not increment it. Working days 3, 4, 5 and 6 are calendar days 5, 6, 7 and 8. Their rules remain cumulative. The third-day letter enforces referral age, the fourth introduces specialist certificates, the fifth enforces authenticity, and the sixth introduces bribery. The working-day number is derived and has no separate save field.

Each visit stores `referral` with exactly integer `ageDays` (0–10), `seal` (`clear`, `missing`, `unclear`), boolean `authentic`, and the employer's deterministic local `phone` string. Age is relative to the appointment's calendar day; subtraction supports issue dates before the game's opening date. Ages above three require refusal from working day 3. Before working day 5 seals are clear; clear implies authentic, missing implies inauthentic, and unclear stores its already-drawn truth. A taken referral copies these facts into `paper.details`, which must match its active visit. Phone strings match `employerPhone(patientId)`; they are game data and never URLs or network destinations. Generation draws about 5% expired referrals. From day 5, current tuning allocates 80% clear, 10% missing and 10% unclear seals; an unclear seal is independently authentic with probability 50%.

A visit's `specialist` is null or exactly `{ jobId, phone }`, with a catalog job ID and `specialistPhone(patientId)`. It is available from working day 4 only for a hypertensive catalog case. Generation chooses 25% of hypertensive patients, then a mismatched job in one third of those certificates. `specialistTaken` records whether its separate paper was fetched; it cannot be true without a certificate. Its paper is `{ id, kind: "specialist", day, patientId, location, certificate: { jobId, phone } }`, unique by day/patient, and retains the same certificate as the active visit. It can be fetched before taking the referral. Validity means its job matches the patient's job; it waives only the hypertension contraindication and never an invalid referral, a vision contraindication or an abnormal general examination.

Visits and notes carry `calls: { specialist, employer }`, with each conclusion null before calling or a boolean result afterwards. Specialist calls require working day 4, a taken certificate and a result matching its job; employer calls require working day 5, a taken referral and a result matching its saved authenticity. Conclusions automatically copy into all same-visit notes, including newly fetched notes and filed sheets. Phone-only notes are valid without a clinical examination. Conclusions remain after discarding the corresponding source document. Dialogue/dialing stage, sound and transient call captions are presentation state and restart neither a clinical outcome nor a random draw on import.

Official mail is `{ id, kind: "directive", day, patientId: null, location, sender, rule, opened }`. Rules are `dates`, `specialist` and `authenticity`; sender is `chamber` for `specialist` and `zus` otherwise. Directives are unique for their exact scheduled calendar day. Opening, filing, clipping and discarding use the ordinary paper mechanisms; discarded directives are not re-created on import. New-day creation checks inventory bounds before advancing.

`visit.bribe` is `none`, `offered`, `accepted` or `returned`. Non-none states require working day 6 or later. The scripted second patient on day 6 is the queue-prefix cap exception; subsequent offers are drawn for approximately a third of medically unfit patients. The offer stores clear authentic, unexpired referral facts and no specialist certificate. `effectivePatient(patientId, true)` supplies the reproducible borderline variant: normal general findings, either vision −6.25/−2 or pressure 162/101, and normal values for the other test. It retains the immutable identity, employer, job and body measurements. Notes persist `bribe: boolean` so archived clinical findings validate against the same variant after the visit ends. Pressure variation uses this effective baseline and the existing first-two-history rules. A present visit's notes must agree with its bribery status and calls.

In versions 16–25, taking cash added 100 zł to `money` immediately and returning it subtracted that amount. Version 26 defers newly accepted cash until day settlement (see below). Since v24, the daily net `bribeIncome` journal separates that cash from the opening balance and includes it in reported income, without paying it twice. Versions 16–23 included this cash in the opening balance. Approval of a bribing patient uses 30% ZUS detection; normal approvals despite document/medical contraindications retain 65%. An otherwise eligible patient wrongly refused because of an unclear genuine seal also uses the ZUS 65% detection and fine path; other mistaken refusals keep employer/insurance consequences. `ending` is normally null. Accepting cash and returning a refusal sets `assault-pending` in the cabinet after the completed visit. Settlement, including forced 17:15 closing, changes it to `hospital` in the home phase. The assault and hospital endings require at least one completed patient and working day 6 or later. Bankruptcy can occur from the first day, including a day with no completed visits. Hospital is terminal, blocks `next-day`, and still preserves the valid settled ledger. The ending's narrative uses the existing generated return-home artwork with a letter overlay. Both pending and final endings survive export/import.

## Append-only notes and bankruptcy (version 18)

Every visit and note has a required `noteOrder` array. Allowed unique entries are `general`, `history`, `vision`, `pressure`, `pressure-repeat`, `measurements`, `call-specialist` and `call-employer`. Each entry requires its corresponding saved result; history requires the general examination, and `pressure-repeat` requires two recorded measurements. The second pressure entry must follow the first, and history must follow the general result. The visit’s clinical entries must agree with `examinationOrder`; that original field still records first-performance order for clinical validation. The journal and all other fields retain exact-key validation. Each sheet owns an independent copied array.

The general examination appends `general` and `history` once. A first pressure measurement appends `pressure`; a second appends `pressure-repeat` at the current end, even if other procedures occurred between them. Later pressure readings change only the latest transient result. A phone conclusion appends once. Notes receive these entries automatically, including in the drawer. Later-fetched sheets copy the complete visit journal. An older partial note keeps its own existing prefix and appends missing results, so its journal may differ from a newly fetched complete sheet. Rendering and accessible descriptions use the same ordered entries; neither mutates the journal. `additionalWritten` remains a boolean compatibility field, set by the general examination in new play; history display depends on the actual general result and journal entry, never on a pen action.

`ending` additionally accepts `bankruptcy`, only in the home phase with a negative validated closing balance. A home state below zero cannot have a null ending. Settlement checks income and expenses before choosing the ending; exactly zero remains playable. A pending assault takes precedence and becomes `hospital`. Both terminal states block actions and next-day transitions. Bankruptcy presents the final budget and cloakroom epilogue, with a direct full restart that retains preferences.

Versions 1–17 are validated against their original exact schemas before migration. Their note journals are initialized once in their previously displayed order: clinical entries with pressure readings grouped, then history when a general result exists, then phone conclusions. Older formats did not record the second reading’s precise time, so migration does not invent it. Subsequent additions go to the end. Clinical findings, money, ledger, counters and RNG remain unchanged. An older settled negative balance with no ending gains `bankruptcy`; an existing hospital ending is preserved. An older active visit is not settled during import. Version 18 does not apply missing-field defaults to malformed current files.

## Bribe income (version 24)

The state and every settlement, including `budgetHistory`, require `bribeIncome`: an integer multiple of 100 in −10,300…10,300. In v24–25, taking cash added 100 to both money and the journal; returning it subtracted 100 once. Version 26 retains the journal and defers newly accepted cash payment, as described below. A return of cash accepted on an earlier day or in an older save can produce a negative daily journal. A new day resets it to zero. Interrupted actions do not alter it.

`income = basePay + patientBonus + bribeIncome`; `openingBalance = cash before settlement − bribeIncome`; `closingBalance = openingBalance + income − expenses`. This displays the actual opening cash and bribe income separately while keeping the existing wage payout, expense calculations and ending checks. Friday’s journal remains in the weekend history.
