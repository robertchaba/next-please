# Patient and occupation catalog

The client-side catalog contains **188 portraits with five authored identities each: 940 active identities and 5,640 clinical cases**. Every identity has a fixed name, occupation, employer, birth date and sex, plus five original finding variants (healthy, vision, pressure, combined, general) and one appended discretionary variant. The original 553 cases remain available for saved queues and paperwork, giving 6,193 stable case records in total. See the [complete illustrated identity table](patient-identities.md) for all names, professions and starting ages.

`src/core/patient-identities.ts` supplies five explicit age/job pairs per portrait. `PATIENT_IDENTITIES` in `src/core/patients.ts` resolves stable demographics and compatible employers; both dictionaries in `src/i18n.ts` contain all names and job labels, including feminine Polish forms for new identities. Age stays within three years of the portrait's original art brief. Occupations were selected against the actual illustrations, considering clothing, presentation and age; scholarly portraits include professor, teacher, editor, librarian and engineer roles, while work shirts and workshop jackets support technical trades. No profession is selected by a numeric rotation. Clinical findings never change the chosen identity.

## Stable records and saved paperwork

Patient case IDs are array indices and are append-only. IDs 0–102 preserve their existing employer, job, sex, measurements, findings and expected decision. The new profiles do not reinterpret those historical cases. IDs 103–302 supply the first eight variants of character IDs 28–52. IDs 303–327 append a general-examination concern for each of those 25 identities. IDs 328–552 append nine variants for each of the newest 25 identities (eight ordinary variants across two jobs, then one general-examination concern). All fields of IDs 0–552 remain identical. IDs 553–2502 append five clinical cases per new identity. `patientIdentity` resolves a new case to its identity; historical cases retain their original clinical copy, with the user-requested 1.0.8 name-label corrections applied to 19 portraits on 1 November 2026 (see the identity table). `characterId` remains the stable visual group (0–187), not the new identity index (0–939); `portraitId` continues to select the image. The saved case ID uniquely identifies the person and findings without a redundant save field. Existing cases retain their prior expected decisions; numeric vision and pressure values are deterministic representations consistent with their original pass/refer outcomes. Each identity's birth date, sex, portrait, occupation, employer and body measurements stay fixed across its new cases. Different identities sharing a portrait have different names and ages. Names, occupations, employers, duty descriptions, exposure descriptions and supplementary notes are localized in both dictionaries in `src/i18n.ts` and indexed by the saved case ID.

Historical birth dates match the portrait's intended age on 3 November 1983; the five new identities vary that age by at most three years. The three 25-portrait batches retain the ages recorded in their original [first](art-economy-2026-10-25.md), [second](art-patients-expansion-2026-10-25.md) and [third](art-patients-2026-10-26.md) generation records. The original three birth years were corrected on 26 October 2026 to match their middle-aged/middle-aged/older art direction: 1942-04-18 (41), 1937-11-06 (45), and 1925-08-23 (58). This user-requested demographic correction preserves case IDs and clinical findings; dates were never stored independently in saves. `patientAge` counts completed years from the birth date and a supplied civil date, including whether the birthday has occurred. The note header writes the age calculated for its saved `paper.day`, so a filed note never gains years merely because a later visit is open. Blank sheets remain blank. No host clock is consulted and no redundant age field is persisted.

Do not reorder or remove a case, character, occupation or employer already used by a save. Append records instead. If historical case facts must change, introduce a new case and an explicit versioned migration with evidence that stored findings and decisions remain meaningful.

## Occupations and requirements

There are 48 occupations and 27 employers. The fourth portrait batch adds militia officer, pilot and miner, with a militia station, flight unit and mine. Steelworkers retain the existing furnace-operator occupation (ID 18). The identity expansion appends teacher, professor, librarian, accountant, clerk, engineer, architect, editor, drafter, receptionist, chemist, technician and carpenter, with a university, library, design office and joinery. These use the existing fictional requirement profiles. Earlier additions include typesetting, watch repair, tailoring, baking, cooking, cleaning, gardening, postal delivery, electrical work, welding, furnace work, lathe operation, laboratory work, proofreading, bookbinding, laundry, boiler tending, railway track work, signalling, forklift operation, luggage handling, quality inspection, shift supervision and surveying assistance.

`JOBS` supplies stable IDs and machine-readable keys, a requirement profile, exposure tags, suitable employers and managerial responsibility. The 16 exposure tags describe paper/textile dust, noise, heights, traffic, oil, lifting, stress, fine detail, heat, chemicals, outdoor work, night work, standing, food work and mineral dust. Six work patterns describe daytime, early, shift, night, split and seasonal work. These are structured authoring data for future rules and story. Exposure tags and work patterns do not themselves impose hidden penalties or unlock unimplemented examinations.

The three implemented profiles use intentionally simplified fictional game rules:

| Referral code PL | Referral code EN | Required examinations | Findings that affect the decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WC | V+BP | General, vision, pressure, weight and height | General concerns, significant vision defect and pressure |
| W | V | General, vision, weight and height | General concerns and significant vision defect |
| C | BP | General, pressure, weight and height | General concerns and pressure |

Polish referrals retain “Wymogi: WC/W/C”; English referrals use “Tests: V+BP/V/BP”, with V for vision and BP for blood pressure. Each rulebook explains and consistently uses its localized codes, alongside the common requirement for general examination, weight and height. The stable `full`/`vision`/`pressure` profile IDs, clinical rules and save data do not depend on these labels. General-examination concerns require refusal unless the day-7 psychiatric or chest-X-ray report resolves that specific concern; unrelated concerns remain disqualifying. Pilots and militia officers wear uniforms in every identity variant. From working day 7, additional examinations resolve occupation-specific concerns as described below. The historical `refer` categories feed queue balance; current medical assessment distinguishes `fit`, `unfit` and `discretionary` as described below. Optional vision/pressure tests outside the profile do not change the decision. Body measurements are required documentation, without a numerical fitness threshold. Completing the required examinations is distinct from having made the correct decision; the doctor can still stamp and hand over a certificate early under the existing gameplay rules.

`decisionForFindings` derives the expected decision for a new case. `requiredExaminations` and `hasRequiredExaminations` expose the same profile to game transitions. Numeric vision uses left/right diopters; values greater than 6 in either eye require refusal and exactly 6 is discretionary. Numeric blood pressure is discretionary within ±5 of systolic 160 or diastolic 100, provided neither component exceeds the upper edge. Values above that edge require refusal for the relevant profiles. Values at systolic 180 or diastolic 110 are highlighted red. These tuning values are fictional game parameters, not clinical diagnostic criteria; changing them requires keeping case outcomes, save migration and both rulebooks consistent. Rendering reads the catalog and draws the localized profile code and recorded numeric findings; it does not assess cases or mutate state. Original cases retain the WC profile and their recorded findings; restored visits use the current decision rules.

Abnormal general examinations name one of 18 specific physical findings, or the occupation-specific psychiatric/radiological concern, in both languages, rather than only stating that an abnormality exists. The findings include heart murmur, irregular pulse, wheezing, crackles at lung bases, lower-leg swelling, hand tremor, impaired balance, weak hand grip, restricted shoulder movement, left-leg weakness, reduced finger sensation, tenderness under the right ribs, enlarged liver, enlarged neck lymph nodes, breathlessness on light exertion, bluish lips, marked hoarseness and a swollen knee joint. These findings remain disqualifying, apart from the specific day-7 concern mapping below; healthy results remain “bez uwag” / “no concerns”. `generalFindingIndex` derives the stable variant from the immutable abnormal case ID modulo the fixed `GENERAL_FINDING_COUNT` of 18. Keep this mapping and dictionary order stable when extending the catalog. `generalReading` supplies the localized text to notes and accessible examination descriptions. Repeating a test, filing a note, changing language or importing a save cannot reroll it; no new random draw or save field is required.

## Queue construction

`createRoster(seed)` shuffles the eligible visual character groups (all 188 by default) and selects one of their 25 new cases (five identities × five findings). Each face occurs once per daily queue, and all five identities have equal initial selection probability. Historical IDs 0–552 are import-only and do not appear in freshly generated queues. `createDailyRoster(seed, day, portraitHistory)` filters out pilot, militia-officer, miner and furnace-operator identities before working day 7. Portraits with no eligible identity are omitted; remaining civilian portraits can use their other authored professions. Early queues therefore contain 168 distinct portraits. All 4,700 active cases and 188 portraits become eligible on working day 7.

New queues cap unfit cases at **33%**, rounding down: at most **55 of 168** before working day 7 and **62 of 188 (32.98%)** from then onward. If necessary, seeded draws replace excess unfit findings with existing fit cases of the **same identity**, keeping the selected name, profession, employer and birth date intact. All new identities have at least one fit variant. Historical cases are never rewritten.

The limit also holds for every prefix: among the first `n` patients, at most `floor(33 * n / 100)` are unfit. When an unfit patient would exceed that limit, the next fit patient moves ahead of them; the remaining order is preserved. This protects the portion actually seen before admissions close, rather than only the full queue. In particular, the first three patients are fit, but their identities and cases remain random; exactly one in three would be 33.33%, exceeding the requested limit. The same input seed reproduces the final queue and updated random state. The legacy second argument remains accepted for call compatibility and does not reserve particular introductory identities.

Before this balance change, 218 of the 328 catalog cases were unfit (66.46%). The actual expected daily share was higher: **68.71%**, or 36.4167 of 53, calculated by summing each identity's unfit-case fraction because identities have different numbers of variants. A simulation of seeds 0–9999 produced 68.6953%. These are game-balance figures, not population health statistics.

`GameState.portraitHistory` records unique admitted character IDs, oldest appearance first. At the next workday, unseen faces retain their seeded shuffle and precede all seen faces; seen faces follow in oldest-first order. Only admission updates this history, including a visit later interrupted at closing. An uncalled position in a long daily queue does not count as seen. Returning from an additional examination continues the existing visit and does not update history. The bounded list survives weekends and save import/export. With admission history present, prefix balancing selects a fit clinical variant of the same identity instead of moving a familiar face ahead. The sixth-day scripted bribe likewise changes the second patient's medical variant without changing their face, name, age or job. Pending return visits still retain their established schedule and findings.

The game creates a fresh seed for a new game, and its save contract persists the exact roster and random state. An imported historical queue remains intact until the next queue is created. The caller owns random seeding; no renderer accesses browser randomness.

## Adding content

1. Append the identity's name in both locale tables and author age/job data against its portrait. For a new portrait, generate it with the built-in image generator, retain its source and prompt, and prepare its runtime sprite using the project art workflow.
2. Append any new occupation and employer IDs; provide every visible field in both dictionaries. Keep occupations and names within the existing native document width.
3. Add cases with stable IDs, appropriate employer/job pairings, consistent identity fields, and an explicit requirement profile. Use `decisionForFindings` for the expected decision.
4. For a new implemented requirement, extend the shared profile data, examinations and save validation as needed; explain it in both rulebooks before enabling its consequences. Data-only future exposures must remain mechanically inactive until then.
5. When extending catalog or queue bounds, advance the save version and retain the historical case/identity limits in old validators. Keep imported queues unchanged until the next workday.
6. Run the catalog, paperwork and font tests, then the project build and relevant gameplay/save/browser checks. The referral has at most ten wrapped lines at its native 166-pixel text width; the profile shares its existing managerial row so seals and signatures remain clear.

`tests/patients.test.ts` verifies catalog coverage, stable historical outcomes, compatible new employers, profile-dependent decisions and examinations, seeded replay, unique identities and the 33% cap for every queue prefix across 4098 seeds. The sampled queues also cover every active case. The identity tests check all 940 names, age ranges, fixed jobs, suitable employers and scholarly portrait roles. Historical case digests protect all 553 previous records. Save tests preserve an old queue above the cap and verify that the next workday receives a balanced queue without changing import validation. `tests/paperwork.test.ts` and `tests/font.test.ts` check both dictionaries, Polish glyphs and native document/rulebook bounds.

The third portrait batch also has a frozen digest of all 328 prior case records, v12-to-v13 preservation checks, round trips for all 25 new identities and a browser check that displays every new portrait through the public import flow.

Save v15 expanded the case bounds to 2502 while v13/v14 stayed capped at 552. Current tests round-trip all 940 active identities and preserve historical queues and filed notes. Browser checks exercise all five identities for academic, workshop and female professional portraits, including PL/EN labels, ages and unchanged portrait pixels.

## Discretionary variants and story design (0.8.5)

IDs 5253–6192 append one alternative per identity: `5253 + identityId`. The original IDs and objects remain unchanged; `patientIdentity` resolves both ranges. Alternatives use exactly 6 diopters, a 160/100 baseline with fluctuating repeat readings, or an equivocal occupation-specific specialist report. Ordinary physical symptoms are not waived: the new case supplies its own consistent findings. Psychiatric/miner concerns remain until the relevant report returns; equivocal audiometry/vestibular cases have no unrelated general-examination contraindication.

New queue generation counts naturally borderline old cases and fills towards approximately 20% of the old refusal pool by choosing these alternatives, preserving the selected identity and prefix cap. `expectedDecision: 'unfit'` on the alternatives is the conservative queue category, not a forced stamp. Use `expectedVisitDecision` for actual decisions and `additionalAssessment` for specialist wording and meaning. A received equivocal report or an applicable numerical borderline permits either stamp with no ZUS/employer decision consequence. An independent clear contraindication or invalid referral still requires refusal; report attachment, filing and bribe-return duties remain independent.

For narrative scenes, choose the case ID and preserve it across deferred visits and saves; do not reroll its opinion during rendering or dialogue. Keep ambiguity in the clinical description and the doctor's consideration of history, general health and work conditions. Do not expose probabilities or immunity from penalties in player-facing prose. The bit stored in specialist reports and journal tokens is interpreted together with the immutable case; [save v30](save-format.md#version-30-discretionary-medical-decisions) documents that contract and the frozen v29 ceiling.
