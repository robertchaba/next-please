# Patient and occupation catalog

The client-side catalog contains **113 portraits with five authored identities each: 565 active identities and 2,825 clinical cases**. Every identity has a fixed name, occupation, employer, birth date and sex, plus five independent finding variants (healthy, vision, pressure, combined, general). The original 553 cases remain available for saved queues and paperwork, giving 3,378 stable case records in total. See the [complete illustrated identity table](patient-identities.md) for all names, professions and starting ages.

`src/core/patient-identities.ts` supplies five explicit age/job pairs per portrait. `PATIENT_IDENTITIES` in `src/core/patients.ts` resolves stable demographics and compatible employers; both dictionaries in `src/i18n.ts` contain all names and job labels, including feminine Polish forms for new identities. Age stays within three years of the portrait's original art brief. Occupations were selected against the actual illustrations, considering clothing, presentation and age; scholarly portraits include professor, teacher, editor, librarian and engineer roles, while work shirts and workshop jackets support technical trades. No profession is selected by a numeric rotation. Clinical findings never change the chosen identity.

## Stable records and saved paperwork

Patient case IDs are array indices and are append-only. IDs 0–102 preserve their existing employer, job, sex, measurements, findings and expected decision. The new profiles do not reinterpret those historical cases. IDs 103–302 supply the first eight variants of character IDs 28–52. IDs 303–327 append a general-examination concern for each of those 25 identities. IDs 328–552 append nine variants for each of the newest 25 identities (eight ordinary variants across two jobs, then one general-examination concern). All fields of IDs 0–552 remain identical. IDs 553–2502 append five clinical cases per new identity. `patientIdentity` resolves a new case to its identity; historical cases retain their original clinical copy, with user-requested name-label corrections applied to ten portraits on 26 October 2026 (see the identity table). `characterId` remains the stable visual group (0–112), not the new identity index (0–564); `portraitId` continues to select the image. The saved case ID uniquely identifies the person and findings without a redundant save field. Existing cases retain their prior expected decisions; numeric vision and pressure values are deterministic representations consistent with their original pass/refer outcomes. Each identity's birth date, sex, portrait, occupation, employer and body measurements stay fixed across its new cases. Different identities sharing a portrait have different names and ages. Names, occupations, employers, duty descriptions, exposure descriptions and supplementary notes are localized in both dictionaries in `src/i18n.ts` and indexed by the saved case ID.

Historical birth dates match the portrait's intended age on 3 November 1983; the five new identities vary that age by at most three years. The three 25-portrait batches retain the ages recorded in their original [first](art-economy-2026-10-25.md), [second](art-patients-expansion-2026-10-25.md) and [third](art-patients-2026-10-26.md) generation records. The original three birth years were corrected on 26 October 2026 to match their middle-aged/middle-aged/older art direction: 1942-04-18 (41), 1937-11-06 (45), and 1925-08-23 (58). This user-requested demographic correction preserves case IDs and clinical findings; dates were never stored independently in saves. `patientAge` counts completed years from the birth date and a supplied civil date, including whether the birthday has occurred. The note header writes the age calculated for its saved `paper.day`, so a filed note never gains years merely because a later visit is open. Blank sheets remain blank. No host clock is consulted and no redundant age field is persisted.

Do not reorder or remove a case, character, occupation or employer already used by a save. Append records instead. If historical case facts must change, introduce a new case and an explicit versioned migration with evidence that stored findings and decisions remain meaningful.

## Occupations and requirements

There are 48 occupations and 27 employers. The fourth portrait batch adds militia officer, pilot and miner, with a militia station, flight unit and mine. Steelworkers retain the existing furnace-operator occupation (ID 18). The identity expansion appends teacher, professor, librarian, accountant, clerk, engineer, architect, editor, drafter, receptionist, chemist, technician and carpenter, with a university, library, design office and joinery. These use the existing fictional requirement profiles. Earlier additions include typesetting, watch repair, tailoring, baking, cooking, cleaning, gardening, postal delivery, electrical work, welding, furnace work, lathe operation, laboratory work, proofreading, bookbinding, laundry, boiler tending, railway track work, signalling, forklift operation, luggage handling, quality inspection, shift supervision and surveying assistance.

`JOBS` supplies stable IDs and machine-readable keys, a requirement profile, exposure tags, suitable employers and managerial responsibility. The 16 exposure tags describe paper/textile dust, noise, heights, traffic, oil, lifting, stress, fine detail, heat, chemicals, outdoor work, night work, standing, food work and mineral dust. Six work patterns describe daytime, early, shift, night, split and seasonal work. These are structured authoring data for future rules and story. Exposure tags and work patterns do not themselves impose hidden penalties or unlock unimplemented examinations.

The three implemented profiles use intentionally simplified fictional game rules:

| Referral code | Required examinations | Findings that affect the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WC | General, vision, pressure, weight and height | General concerns, significant vision defect and pressure |
| W | General, vision, weight and height | General concerns and significant vision defect |
| C | General, pressure, weight and height | General concerns and pressure |

The codes are identical in both interface languages. Their meanings and the common requirement for general examination, weight and height are explained in the rulebook. General-examination concerns require refusal unless the day-7 psychiatric or chest-X-ray report resolves that specific concern; unrelated concerns remain disqualifying. Pilots and militia officers wear uniforms in every identity variant. From working day 7, additional examinations resolve occupation-specific concerns as described below. A `refer` result in a required decision-making test yields `unfit`; all required results passing yields `fit`. Optional vision/pressure tests outside the profile do not change the decision. Body measurements are required documentation, without a numerical fitness threshold. Completing the required examinations is distinct from having made the correct decision; the doctor can still stamp and hand over a certificate early under the existing gameplay rules.

`decisionForFindings` derives the expected decision for a new case. `requiredExaminations` and `hasRequiredExaminations` expose the same profile to game transitions. Numeric vision uses left/right diopters; absolute values of at least 6 in either eye are significant. Numeric blood pressure requires refusal for relevant profiles at systolic 160 or diastolic 100. Values at systolic 180 or diastolic 110 are highlighted red. These tuning values are fictional game parameters, not clinical diagnostic criteria; changing them requires keeping case outcomes, save migration and both rulebooks consistent. Rendering reads the catalog and draws the localized profile code and recorded numeric findings; it does not assess cases or mutate state. Original cases retain the WC profile and their prior decisions.

Abnormal general examinations name one of 18 specific physical findings, or the occupation-specific psychiatric/radiological concern, in both languages, rather than only stating that an abnormality exists. The findings include heart murmur, irregular pulse, wheezing, crackles at lung bases, lower-leg swelling, hand tremor, impaired balance, weak hand grip, restricted shoulder movement, left-leg weakness, reduced finger sensation, tenderness under the right ribs, enlarged liver, enlarged neck lymph nodes, breathlessness on light exertion, bluish lips, marked hoarseness and a swollen knee joint. These findings remain disqualifying, apart from the specific day-7 concern mapping below; healthy results remain “bez uwag” / “no concerns”. `generalFindingIndex` derives the stable variant from the immutable abnormal case ID modulo the fixed `GENERAL_FINDING_COUNT` of 18. Keep this mapping and dictionary order stable when extending the catalog. `generalReading` supplies the localized text to notes and accessible examination descriptions. Repeating a test, filing a note, changing language or importing a save cannot reroll it; no new random draw or save field is required.

## Queue construction

`createRoster(seed)` shuffles the eligible visual character groups (all 113 by default) and selects one of their 25 new cases (five identities × five findings). Each face occurs once per daily queue, and all five identities have equal initial selection probability. Historical IDs 0–552 are import-only and do not appear in freshly generated queues. `createDailyRoster(seed, day)` filters out pilot, militia-officer, miner and furnace-operator identities before working day 7. Portraits with no eligible identity are omitted; remaining civilian portraits can use their other authored professions. Early queues therefore contain 93 distinct portraits. All 2,825 active cases and 113 portraits become eligible on working day 7.

New queues cap unfit cases at **33%**, rounding down: at most **30 of 93** before working day 7 and **37 of 113 (32.74%)** from then onward. If necessary, seeded draws replace excess unfit findings with existing fit cases of the **same identity**, keeping the selected name, profession, employer and birth date intact. All new identities have at least one fit variant. Historical cases are never rewritten.

The limit also holds for every prefix: among the first `n` patients, at most `floor(33 * n / 100)` are unfit. When an unfit patient would exceed that limit, the next fit patient moves ahead of them; the remaining order is preserved. This protects the portion actually seen before admissions close, rather than only the full queue. In particular, the first three patients are fit, but their identities and cases remain random; exactly one in three would be 33.33%, exceeding the requested limit. The same input seed reproduces the final queue and updated random state. The legacy second argument remains accepted for call compatibility and does not reserve particular introductory identities.

Before this balance change, 218 of the 328 catalog cases were unfit (66.46%). The actual expected daily share was higher: **68.71%**, or 36.4167 of 53, calculated by summing each identity's unfit-case fraction because identities have different numbers of variants. A simulation of seeds 0–9999 produced 68.6953%. These are game-balance figures, not population health statistics.

The game creates a fresh seed for a new game, and its save contract persists the exact roster and random state. An imported historical queue remains intact until the next queue is created. The caller owns random seeding; no renderer accesses browser randomness.

## Adding content

1. Append the identity's name in both locale tables and author age/job data against its portrait. For a new portrait, generate it with the built-in image generator, retain its source and prompt, and prepare its runtime sprite using the project art workflow.
2. Append any new occupation and employer IDs; provide every visible field in both dictionaries. Keep occupations and names within the existing native document width.
3. Add cases with stable IDs, appropriate employer/job pairings, consistent identity fields, and an explicit requirement profile. Use `decisionForFindings` for the expected decision.
4. For a new implemented requirement, extend the shared profile data, examinations and save validation as needed; explain it in both rulebooks before enabling its consequences. Data-only future exposures must remain mechanically inactive until then.
5. When extending catalog or queue bounds, advance the save version and retain the historical case/identity limits in old validators. Keep imported queues unchanged until the next workday.
6. Run the catalog, paperwork and font tests, then the project build and relevant gameplay/save/browser checks. The referral has at most ten wrapped lines at its native 166-pixel text width; the profile shares its existing managerial row so seals and signatures remain clear.

`tests/patients.test.ts` verifies catalog coverage, stable historical outcomes, compatible new employers, profile-dependent decisions and examinations, seeded replay, unique identities and the 33% cap for every queue prefix across 4098 seeds. The sampled queues also cover every active case. The identity tests check all 565 names, age ranges, fixed jobs, suitable employers and scholarly portrait roles. Historical case digests protect all 553 previous records. Save tests preserve an old queue above the cap and verify that the next workday receives a balanced queue without changing import validation. `tests/paperwork.test.ts` and `tests/font.test.ts` check both dictionaries, Polish glyphs and native document/rulebook bounds.

The third portrait batch also has a frozen digest of all 328 prior case records, v12-to-v13 preservation checks, round trips for all 25 new identities and a browser check that displays every new portrait through the public import flow.

Save v15 expanded the case bounds to 2502 while v13/v14 stayed capped at 552. Current tests round-trip all 565 active identities and preserve historical queues and filed notes. Browser checks exercise all five identities for academic, workshop and female professional portraits, including PL/EN labels, ages and unchanged portrait pixels.

## Day-seven examinations and portraits 76–100

The appended 625 cases, IDs 2503–3127, add 125 identities for 25 new portraits. All 2,503 previous records retain every field. Ten militia portraits and five pilot portraits keep their illustrated profession across all five identities. Three civilian portraits provide steelworkers and two provide miners; the five remaining civilian portraits have varied suitable occupations. Generation source, prompts, preparation and review are recorded in [the fourth-batch artwork notes](art-patients-76-100-2026-10-26.md).

The day-7 examination mapping uses job 18 for tonal audiometry, 46 for a vestibular examination, 45 for psychiatric consultation and 47 for chest X-ray. Psychiatric and radiological concerns are shown explicitly for the relevant abnormal general cases. A matching clear report resolves only that concern; it does not waive eyesight, pressure, other medical findings, invalid dates or seals. The ordinary requirement profiles and stable catalog findings remain available for historical imports. Referral eligibility, returned reports and actual visit decisions live in the progression/additional-examination logic, outside drawing.

Version 19 accepts cases through 3127 and newly constructed 103-person queues; versions 15–18 retain the frozen 2502 bound. Historical 28-, 53- and 78-person queues remain intact on import. A returning patient resumes the deferred encounter with its saved findings and paperwork, and does not constitute a newly selected identity or an extra completed-visit bonus.

Pending return visits keep their established identity and findings when a new day’s queue is created. The freshly drawn case for that portrait is replaced by its pending encounter; continuation visits are not rerolled to satisfy the fresh-case fitness balance.

Version 21 supports shorter queues without renumbering characters or changing existing cases. Versions 1–20 keep their original validation and imported queues; the occupation gate applies when the next daily queue is generated.
